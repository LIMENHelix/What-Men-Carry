# Alcoholism and Addiction — Dossier

## THE FACTS

**Prevalence:**
- 12.9% of men ages 18+ had an alcohol use disorder (AUD) in the past year (2024)
- Roughly 20% of men have an alcohol use disorder, compared to 7–12% of women
- 11.5% of men and boys over 12 have a substance use disorder (vs. 6.4% of women)
- Men are 3x more likely than women to die from alcohol-related causes

**Binge drinking:**
- 22% of people aged 21+ reported binge drinking in the last 30 days (2024)
- Men comprise the majority of binge drinkers and heavy users

**Drug use patterns:**
- Men are more likely to abuse illicit drugs and alcohol than women
- Opioid addiction rates are rising among men, particularly in rural areas
- Men are less likely to seek addiction treatment despite higher prevalence

**Mortality and morbidity:**
- Globally, 2 million deaths annually attributed to alcohol use among men
- 0.4 million deaths from drug use among men
- Alcohol and drug use contribute to depression, liver disease, cardiovascular disease, and suicide

**Gateway dynamics:**
- Addiction often begins as coping for untreated mental health issues (depression, anxiety, PTSD)
- Financial stress, work stress, and relationship breakdown trigger or worsen substance abuse
- The man may not see it as addiction; he sees it as the only thing that works

The first drink to take the edge off. Then the second drink becomes the edge itself.

The morning after where the memory is blurry and the shame is clear.

The wife who finds the empty bottles in the garage, the lies about what happened.

The DUI that changes everything and nothing.

The job he shows up to hung over, the performance of sobriety.

The friend who stops calling because the version of him that shows up now is just the drinking.

The cost of it—the money, the opportunities, the self-respect.

The way his hands shake in the morning, the Bloody Mary to stop the shaking.

The meeting he doesn't go to because admitting the problem out loud is admitting he's failed.

The kids who learn not to make noise when Dad's drinking.

The night he almost drives his family off the road and no one mentions it the next day.

The knowledge that he could stop, that he's not weak, that the truth is more complicated than that.

## THE MISDIRECTION

What gets mistaken for:

- **Weakness:** He's weak-willed. He lacks discipline. He should just stop.
- **Moral failure:** He's a bad person. He's selfish. He's a drunk.
- **Choice:** He chose this. He likes being drunk. He prefers alcohol to his family.
- **Laziness:** He's using substances as an excuse. He's avoiding responsibility.
- **Bad parenting:** He's damaging his kids. He's a terrible father. He's irresponsible.
- **Simple solution:** He just needs to go to rehab. He just needs to want it badly enough.

No one sees the man using the only tool he has for the pain he cannot name.
