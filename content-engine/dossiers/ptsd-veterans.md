# PTSD — Veterans Dossier

## THE FACTS

**Prevalence:**
- About 7% of all veterans experience PTSD at some point; 6% of the general civilian population
- More recent data (2019-2020): Lifetime PTSD prevalence 9.4%, past-month PTSD 5.0% among veterans
- Combat-exposed veterans: 29% will develop PTSD at some point in their lives

**Service-connected disability (2024):**
- 1,589,833 service-connected veterans receive disability payments for PTSD (56% of all mental health disabilities)
- 81,968 new PTSD service-connected disabilities awarded in FY2024

**Gender patterns:**
- Female veterans are more than twice as likely to experience PTSD (13%) vs. male veterans (6%)
- However, in absolute numbers, male veterans represent the vast majority of PTSD cases due to higher overall veteran population

**Mortality and comorbidity:**
- Veterans with PTSD have elevated rates of suicide, substance abuse, and cardiovascular disease
- Hyperarousal and sleep disturbance are hallmark symptoms affecting every aspect of life

**Treatment gaps:**
- Many veterans do not seek or receive PTSD treatment despite its availability through the VA
- Stigma around "weakness" and self-reliance remain major barriers

## THE LIVED TEXTURE

The veteran who sleeps in clothes so he can move faster if the door opens.

The sudden sound—a car backfiring, a door slamming—and his body is not in a kitchen anymore.

The way he scans a room before he sits down. Where's the exit. Who's behind him. What's the angle.

The shower he takes with the curtain open, the bathroom door unlocked, because enclosed spaces feel like traps now.

The relationships that end because how do you explain that you flinched when he touched your shoulder, and you can't touch anyone without checking first if they have two hands and both eyes.

The job he left because the fluorescent lights triggered the migraines, and the migraines triggered the cascade.

The morning ritual: coffee, a cigarette, checking that his keys are where he left them, checking that everyone in the house is breathing.

The trust he gave his country; the trust he can't give back to anyone—not even to peace.

The man at a wedding who sits at the back and doesn't make eye contact because crowds feel like ambush.

The way he keeps his hands where everyone can see them, a habit from a place where hidden hands meant death.

## THE MISDIRECTION

What gets mistaken for:

- **Aggression:** He's short-tempered. He's always been intense. He needs to chill out.
- **Antisocial behavior:** He won't go to parties. He's withdrawn. He's lost interest in people.
- **Substance abuse:** He drinks too much. He's an alcoholic. He's got an addiction problem.
- **Control issues:** He's paranoid. He doesn't trust anyone. He's controlling and possessive.
- **Avoidance:** He won't talk about his time overseas. He's shut down. He's cold.
- **Sleep problems:** He's lazy. He doesn't sleep because of his bad habits. He should just get a good sleep routine.

People see the behavior and not the nervous system still at war.
