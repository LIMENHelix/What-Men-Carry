# Divorce — Dossier

## THE FACTS

**Prevalence:**
- U.S. divorce rate: 2.3 per 1,000 people (2024), down from 3.6 per 1,000 in 2010
- Approximately 69% of divorces are initiated by women

**Custody outcomes:**
- 78% of custodial parents are mothers; 22% are fathers (Census 2022)
- 90% of divorced mothers retain primary custody of their children
- This represents a shift: custodial fathers have increased from 16% in 1994 to 21.8% in 2022

**Financial impact on fathers:**
- 79% of custodial mothers receive a child support award
- Only 30% of custodial fathers receive child support from the other parent
- Men lose an average of significant household income post-divorce, affecting their ability to remarry or rebuild

**Mental health impact:**
- Divorce is linked to elevated rates of depression, substance abuse, and suicide
- Men are significantly less likely to seek support during divorce proceedings
- Social isolation increases dramatically post-divorce for men compared to women

**Visitation and parental connection:**
- Men with non-custodial status often struggle to maintain consistent connection
- Court-ordered visitation schedules can be rigid and emotionally painful
- Many fathers report feeling like "visiting parents" rather than active parents

## THE LIVED TEXTURE

The day he left with two suitcases and the understanding that he would see his kids on weekends, if that.

The apartment he rents, empty on the walls, empty in the ways that matter.

The Sunday evening when he has to give them back—the ritual of the handoff, the small faces in the car window.

The week he doesn't see them, checking his phone for messages that might not come.

The school play he finds out about from the school website, not from her, not from them.

The Christmas morning in a hotel because his custody agreement says he gets them 48 hours, starting tomorrow.

The man at work who does the calculation every day: half his income, half his time, none of the life he built.

The new partner he won't introduce to his kids for a long time because what if it doesn't work, what if he has to explain it again.

The bills for her lawyer, his lawyer, the parenting counselor no one wanted to go to.

The son who stops calling because it's awkward now, and the father who doesn't know how to fix that without stepping on the eggshells.

## THE MISDIRECTION

What gets mistaken for:

- **Bitterness:** He's angry at his ex. He's holding a grudge. He needs to let it go.
- **Deadbeat:** He's not interested in his kids. He's avoiding responsibility. He's weak.
- **Moving on too fast:** He's already dating again. He doesn't care. He's shallow.
- **Financial irresponsibility:** He's not paying enough. He's greedy. He's selfish.
- **Bad parenting:** He doesn't know how to raise kids. He's disengaged. He's checked out.
- **Emotional unavailability:** He never talks about it. He's cold. He's unfeeling.

People see the distance and assume he chose it.
