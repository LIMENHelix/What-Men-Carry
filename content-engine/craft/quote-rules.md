# What Men Carry — Quote Standard

A quote must pass ALL FIVE gates. If it fails any one, reject it.

## Gate 1: Never name the issue
No "PTSD," "depression," "suicide," "custody," "divorce," "addiction," "anxiety," "trauma." The word is the wound; we don't say the word. The reader should know by recognition, not label.

## Gate 2: Under 14 words
Shorter hits harder. Count every word. Benchmark: "They sleep because he doesn't." (5 words, unmistakable weight).

## Gate 3: Two readings
Invisible to someone who hasn't lived it. Unmistakable to someone who has. 
- If a stranger could guess the topic in 10 seconds, it's too direct.
- If the man who lived it wouldn't flinch, it's too vague.
- The sweet spot: a small image that only makes sense if you know the wound.

## Gate 4: Concrete image, not abstraction
Built on something you can see, hear, or do — not a feeling or idea.
- ❌ "He carries weight" (abstraction)
- ✅ "He checks the locks twice and the hallway once" (concrete, repeatable, tells you something)
- ✅ "She held his hand. He held the rest." (reversal, concrete)

## Gate 5: Honors him
The quote can name the cost but never pities. He is carrying, not broken. This is not inspiration-porn, and it is not tragedy-porn. It is witness.

## Kill-list (auto-reject any quote containing these words)

❌ journey  
❌ battle  
❌ warrior  
❌ struggle  
❌ healing  
❌ broken  
❌ demons  
❌ darkness  
❌ silence (as a standalone noun)  
❌ mask  
❌ storm  

These words have been worn thin by every grief commodity. We don't use them.

## Suicide dossier EXTRA rule

Quotes drawn from the suicide dossier must land on the side of **still-here** — weight carried, watch kept, the decision to stay. Never romanticize the exit. If a draft reads like "the pain is so beautiful," flag it and reject it. If it reads like "he stays because," accept it.
