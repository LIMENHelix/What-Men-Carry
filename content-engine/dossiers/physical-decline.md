# Physical Decline and Aging — Dossier

## THE FACTS

**Hormonal changes:**
- Testosterone production declines at an average rate of 1–2% per year starting around age 20
- Erectile function gradually declines with age; libido decreases as testosterone levels drop

**Muscle and bone loss:**
- Age-related muscle loss (sarcopenia) leads to decreased strength, mobility, and function
- Bone density decreases, increasing risk of osteoporosis and fractures
- Falls and fall-related injuries become a leading cause of injury and death in older men

**Cardiovascular changes:**
- Risk of heart disease rises with age due to hypertension, cholesterol, and arterial plaque buildup
- Cardiovascular fitness declines without active maintenance

**Cognitive changes:**
- Memory, attention, and problem-solving abilities gradually decline
- Processing speed slows

**Lifestyle impact:**
- A sedentary lifestyle and low testosterone create a vicious cycle: inactivity lowers testosterone, which increases fatigue and depression, which increases inactivity
- Depression is both a symptom and cause of physical decline in aging men

**Psychological impact:**
- Loss of physical capability often translates to loss of identity (especially for men whose identity was tied to physical work or strength)
- Sexual dysfunction can trigger depression and relationship breakdown
- Chronic pain becomes a constant companion

## THE LIVED TEXTURE

The moment he can't open the jar himself.

The stairs that used to be nothing and now require him to pause at the top.

The younger man at work who does the heavy lifting now, the small death of irrelevance.

The morning stiffness that takes an hour to wear off, if it wears off at all.

The thing his body used to do without thought—running, lifting, reaching—that now requires planning and recovery time.

The intimacy that changed, the way his body betrayed him before his mind could make sense of it.

The doctor's visit where she says "for a man your age," and he hears "you're old now."

The young father at the park with his kids, the recognition that he cannot do what he's watching.

The sports he played, the activities he did, the version of himself that was strong—that man is becoming a ghost.

The mirror in the morning, the stranger with his face and his hands and his slowness.

The small ache that won't go away, the fear it might not ever.

## THE MISDIRECTION

What gets mistaken for:

- **Laziness:** He's slowing down. He's not pushing himself. He's gotten soft.
- **Giving up:** He's not even trying anymore. He's given in to old age.
- **Weakness:** He's frail. He's fragile. He's losing it.
- **Disinterest:** He doesn't want to do things anymore. He's withdrawn from life.
- **Depression (solely):** He's just sad about aging. He should work on his mindset.
- **Inevitable:** This is just what happens. There's nothing to be done.

No one recognizes the grief of watching yourself become someone else.
