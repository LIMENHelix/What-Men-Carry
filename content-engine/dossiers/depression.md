# Depression — Dossier

## THE FACTS

**Prevalence and diagnosis:**
- Depression affects men and women at nearly equal rates (13.3% of men vs. 14.4% of women reported depressive symptoms in 2024)
- However, experts estimate actual depression in men is significantly underdiagnosed; many cases go unrecognized because symptoms present differently
- Women are 40% more likely to receive mental health treatment (59.2% of women vs. 42.1% of men in 2024)

**Help-seeking gap:**
- Men show 29% lower psychotherapy use than women, despite experiencing similar or greater depression
- Average delay between symptom onset and treatment in men: 11 years
- Only 17% of men received any mental health treatment or counseling in the past year (2024)

**Male depression presentation:**
- Often manifests as irritability, anger, and risk-taking rather than sadness
- "Male depressive syndrome" is less recognized by both men and clinicians
- Comorbid substance abuse is common (men are 3x more likely to die from alcohol-related causes)

**Impact on functioning:**
- Depression contributes to reduced work productivity, relationship breakdown, and increased suicide risk
- Male suicides are often preceded by unrecognized or untreated depression

**Age patterns:**
- Depression in men can emerge at any age but often peaks in mid-life (45–64)
- Older men show elevated rates as health declines and loss accumulates

## THE LIVED TEXTURE

The morning when getting out of bed feels like it takes three hours, though it only takes five minutes.

The shower he's skipped three days because moving feels heavier than it used to.

The jokes that aren't jokes anymore. The dark humor deployed like a shield.

The way his son asks him a question and he hears it underwater, has to ask for it again.

The irritation at small things—traffic, a phone call, the way someone chews—that he knows isn't about those things.

The work he does on autopilot, showing up and functioning, telling everyone he's fine while his insides feel like they're turning to stone.

The hobbies he used to love—fishing, building things, sitting with friends—that now feel like they require a currency he doesn't have.

The sex he doesn't want but feels obligated to perform, so he doesn't have to explain that it's not about his wife, it's that his body feels like a borrowed thing.

The way he catches himself staring at nothing for forty-five minutes and doesn't remember the time passing.

The weekend morning when he stays in bed longer than he should, not from laziness but from the weight.

## THE MISDIRECTION

What gets mistaken for:

- **Laziness:** He's unmotivated. He should just push through. He's lost his drive.
- **Moodiness:** He's in a bad mood. He's grumpy. He needs to lighten up.
- **Stress:** He's just stressed from work. Once the project ends, he'll snap out of it.
- **Disinterest:** He doesn't care about things anymore. He's lost his passion.
- **Selfishness:** He's withdrawn. He's self-centered. He's being difficult.
- **Alcoholism:** He drinks to cope. It's just a drinking problem.
- **Aging:** He's getting older, slowing down. It's normal.

No one realizes the lights are still on but no one is home.
